Probabilistic Palette Lab

Every sample below is colored by the palette on the left — either a real Brightfield product shader, or one of the shape generators, which is where per-shape rules (size groups, size curves, inheritance) actually bite. One design, one set of settings, many seeds, so the only thing changing between thumbnails is what the palette decided. Colors are chosen by weight, not uniformly; unprinted areas are left black so the shirt shows through — black is never inked. Every decision runs on the seeded RNG at a frozen clock, so the same seed always reproduces the same image. Judge a palette by the sample grid, not by one design.

These settings are shared by the preview and every thumbnail — that is what makes the grid a comparison of color alone. Color mode and the palette-driven color pickers are not listed: the palette owns them, and anything you set there would be overwritten on the next render.

Observed ink by area

Base is the weight you typed; seeded is what generative variation made of it for this seed. The third column counts the units the palette actually assigned — a shader's color uniforms, or the composition's shapes. Area is the honest one: every pixel of the rendered frame bucketed to its nearest palette color, with black counted as exposed shirt rather than ink. The two diverge because neither a gradient stop nor a shape owns an equal share of the canvas.
